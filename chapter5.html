Chapter 5: When Exhaustion Meets Grace

HomeTable of Contents

As Ashley began to experience moments of healing, she expected that strength would return in a steady and predictable way. Instead, she discovered that emotional recovery could be tiring in itself. She had spent so long responding to pressure, caring for others, and trying to keep difficult feelings under control that even ordinary responsibilities sometimes seemed overwhelming. Some mornings she woke already weary, wondering how she would find the energy to face another day.

She began to recognize that she had been measuring her worth by how much she could carry. If she could keep working, helping, answering, and showing up, she believed she was doing well. But the cost of that pattern had become clear. She needed to learn that rest was not a reward reserved for people who had finished every responsibility. Rest was a necessary part of being human, and receiving it did not mean she had abandoned the people she loved.

"My grace is sufficient for you, for My power is made perfect in weakness."

2 Corinthians 12:9

This Scripture began to change the way Ashley understood her limitations. Weakness was not a verdict on her value, and needing help did not mean she had failed. God's grace met her in the places where her own strength was insufficient. That grace did not remove the need to make decisions or take practical steps, but it reminded her that she did not have to depend on her own ability alone.

She began making small changes to the rhythm of her days. Instead of immediately reaching for her phone, she tried to spend a few quiet moments in prayer and Scripture. She learned to pause before agreeing to every request and to consider whether she had the capacity to take something on. When she needed rest, she practiced receiving it without turning it into another reason to criticize herself.

"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want. He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul."

Psalm 23:1–3

The image of the Shepherd caring for His sheep helped Ashley see that rest belonged within a life of faith. Jesus did not invite the weary to prove themselves before coming to Him. He called them to come and receive rest. As she began to release expectations that were never hers to carry, she found that she could serve others with greater honesty and care rather than constantly giving from an empty place.

"Come unto Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

Matthew 11:28

There were still demanding days, and Ashley did not suddenly become free from every anxious thought. But she was learning to recognize when she needed to pause, pray, ask for help, or say no. Rest was becoming an act of trust rather than something she felt she had to earn. That lesson would matter in the next stage of her journey, because even as her outward circumstances began to settle, she would still have to face the thoughts and doubts that had taken root during the hardest seasons.

To the reader who is exhausted:

You do not have to prove your worth by carrying more than you can bear. Ask God for wisdom about what is yours to do and what needs to be released, shared, or approached differently. Make room for rest, seek support when you need it, and remember that weakness does not cancel your purpose. Jesus invites the weary to come to Him. You can receive that invitation today.
