Chapter 4: Healing in the Hidden Place

HomeTable of ContentsBegin Reading

The conversations that had wounded Ashley did not simply vanish, but she was beginning to respond to them differently. Instead of replaying every word until it consumed her thoughts, she was learning to bring the hurt to Jesus and ask what needed attention. Some matters still required time, honest communication, or boundaries. Others were things she could not change. This was the beginning of a deeper understanding: healing was not the same as pretending the wound had never happened.

As the weeks passed, Ashley noticed small changes. There were moments when her mind felt quieter and the pressure in her heart eased. Other days remained difficult, and old disappointments still surfaced. She began to understand that restoration rarely follows a straight line. A difficult day did not erase the progress of the days before it, and a moment of sadness did not mean that God had withdrawn His presence.

"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds."

Psalm 147:3

Ashley had spent a long time hiding pain behind responsibility and a determination to appear strong. Now she was learning to let Jesus into the places she had carefully guarded. She began writing down thoughts that troubled her and examining them beside Scripture. When fear told her that she had been abandoned, she reminded herself of God's faithfulness. When shame tried to convince her that being wounded made her weak, she returned to the truth that the Lord draws near to the brokenhearted.

This practice did not make every painful thought disappear, but it helped her recognize that a feeling did not have to become the final authority over her life. She could name what she was experiencing, ask what it was telling her, and compare that message with the truth of God's Word. She was also learning that spiritual care and practical care could work together. Rest, healthy routines, honest conversations, and support from trustworthy people were not signs of spiritual failure. They were part of taking her needs seriously.

"The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me... He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted."

Isaiah 61:1

Gradually, Ashley noticed that she was sleeping more peacefully and finding moments of genuine joy again. These changes did not mean that every struggle had ended. They were signs that she was making room for care, prayer, and healthier ways of living. She was learning not to measure healing by whether she never felt sadness, but by whether she could meet difficult emotions with honesty, support, and renewed trust in Jesus.

"He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

Psalm 91:1

The secret place was becoming more than a temporary shelter from a painful season. It was teaching Ashley how to live with a deeper dependence on God. She was not being prepared to avoid every future difficulty; she was learning where to turn when difficulty came. As that foundation grew stronger, she began to recognize another truth: she did not have to keep demanding strength from herself when grace was available to sustain her.

To the reader learning to heal:

Healing may be gradual, and it may include days when old feelings return. Do not treat those days as proof that you have failed. Bring your thoughts honestly before God, stay connected to safe and supportive people, and take practical steps that care for your well-being. The Lord is near in the process, not only at the finish line. You can move forward one faithful step at a time.
