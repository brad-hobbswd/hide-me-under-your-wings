Chapter 6: The Storm That Tried to Steal Her Identity

HomeTable of ContentsBegin Reading

Learning to rest gave Ashley room to breathe, but it did not mean every struggle had ended. When the demands around her became quieter, she began to notice thoughts that had been difficult to hear beneath the noise of daily life. Doubts surfaced during ordinary moments, especially when she was tired or remembered painful conversations. The questions were familiar: Was she enough? Had she failed? Did anyone truly see her? The battle that had once seemed to come mainly from outside now appeared in the way she understood herself.

"You are not enough."

"You will never succeed."

"No one sees you."

These thoughts returned often enough that Ashley began to recognize their effect. They made her question decisions, seek approval, and measure her value by the reactions of other people. She had been hurt by words spoken around her, but some of those words had become an inner voice she carried long after the conversations ended. She needed to learn that a painful thought could feel convincing without being true.

"For You formed my inward parts... I will praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."

Psalm 139:13–14

Ashley began answering those thoughts by returning to Scripture. When she felt inadequate, she remembered that her worth was not measured by how perfectly she performed or how consistently others approved of her. When she feared being forgotten, she remembered that God knew her and called her by name. This was not about repeating encouraging words while ignoring reality. It was about allowing God's truth to challenge the conclusions she had drawn from pain.

"Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine."

Isaiah 43:1

She also began to notice how easily her identity had become tied to the opinions of others. Approval could feel reassuring one day and disappear the next. Circumstances changed, relationships shifted, and people did not always understand her choices. If her sense of worth depended on those things, it would remain unsettled. Ashley wanted her identity to rest on something more dependable than changing opinions, so she made time to pray, journal, and reflect on what Scripture said about God's love and faithfulness.

The change was gradual. Doubts did not vanish simply because she had identified them, but she became more able to question them rather than accept them automatically. She could acknowledge insecurity without allowing it to direct every decision. She could receive correction without treating it as proof that she was worthless. She was learning to live from the security of belonging to Jesus instead of constantly trying to earn a place in the eyes of people.

"He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

Psalm 91:1

The refuge Ashley had found in God's presence was now shaping the way she saw herself. Yet healing her inner life would also affect her relationships. As she became more aware of her limits and worth, she would need to respond when others noticed that she no longer carried every burden or agreed to every expectation. The next challenge would be learning to protect the peace she was rebuilding while remaining compassionate toward the people around her.

To the reader struggling with identity:

Your worth is not established by performance, approval, or another person's opinion. When painful thoughts arise, do not assume they are the truth simply because they feel powerful. Bring them before God, return to what Scripture says, and seek wise support when those thoughts become difficult to carry alone. You are known by the Lord, and your life is not defined by the voices that have wounded you.
