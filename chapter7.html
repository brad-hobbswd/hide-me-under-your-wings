Chapter 7: Covered Even When People Did Not Understand

HomeTable of ContentsBegin Reading

As Ashley began to live with greater honesty about her needs, the people around her started to notice changes. She was no longer available for every request, and she had become more thoughtful about the commitments she accepted. She sometimes chose quiet evenings instead of social plans, and she no longer felt compelled to explain every decision. What felt to Ashley like a necessary part of healing was not always understood by those who had grown accustomed to her constant availability.

Questions began to surface. Some people wondered why she had become quieter or why she seemed to be stepping back. Others interpreted her boundaries as distance or a change in attitude. Ashley felt the familiar urge to explain herself until everyone understood. She wanted to be seen as caring, not selfish, and she worried that people might mistake her need for space as a lack of love.

"You hide them in the secret place of Your presence from the plots of men."

Psalm 31:20

As she prayed, Ashley began to understand that healing did not require universal approval. She could listen when someone raised a genuine concern, take responsibility when she had caused harm, and still recognize that she could not control every interpretation of her choices. Not every person would understand the season she was walking through, and she did not have to turn every misunderstanding into a debate.

She also had to examine her own motives. A boundary could be healthy when it protected time, safety, or emotional capacity, but it could become an excuse to avoid every difficult conversation. Ashley wanted to be careful not to confuse peace with avoidance. When a relationship mattered and a conversation could be approached safely, honesty and humility were still important. When a situation was harmful, she could seek support and maintain appropriate distance. Wisdom meant knowing the difference.

"Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life."

Proverbs 4:23

That verse helped Ashley see that guarding her heart was not the same as hardening it. She could remain kind without agreeing to everything. She could love people without taking responsibility for every emotion they experienced. She could forgive while allowing trust to be rebuilt through consistent actions over time. Her goal was not to shut people out, but to stop abandoning her own well-being in an effort to keep everyone else comfortable.

Some people understood. Others did not. Ashley had to accept that she could communicate with care and still be misunderstood. She kept asking Jesus to shape her responses so that her boundaries would be guided by wisdom rather than resentment. The process was not always easy, but she was learning that protecting peace and practicing love did not have to be opposing choices.

"No weapon formed against you shall prosper."

Isaiah 54:17

By this point, Ashley's healing was no longer only about what happened inside her. It was changing how she lived, related to others, and responded to pressure. Even so, she would discover that progress did not mean she would never have another difficult moment. Old memories could return, and emotions she thought she had settled could rise again. The next chapter would meet her in one of those moments and show why a setback does not have to erase the work God has already begun.

To the reader who feels misunderstood:

You can be compassionate and still have boundaries. You can listen, apologize, and make changes where needed without surrendering every healthy limit. Ask God to show you when a conversation is needed, when patience is wise, and when distance is necessary. Not everyone will understand your healing, but let your choices be shaped by truth, humility, and love rather than fear of disapproval.
