Chapter 8: Breakdowns That Led to Breakthroughs

HomeTable of ContentsBegin Reading

After learning to protect her peace, Ashley hoped the hardest part of her healing might be behind her. She had become more honest about her limits, more careful with her commitments, and less dependent on the approval of others. Yet one evening, without warning, she found herself overwhelmed by memories and emotions she thought she had already worked through. The experience unsettled her because she had begun to believe that feeling better meant the painful parts of her past would no longer return.

At first, one memory came quietly. Then another followed, bringing with it old disappointment, grief, and questions she had not fully answered. The feelings seemed to gather faster than she could sort through them. Ashley went to her bedroom and sank to the floor, no longer able to hold back the tears. She prayed as honestly as she could, allowing the pain to come into the open instead of trying to push it away again.

"The righteous cry out, and the Lord hears, and delivers them out of all their troubles. The Lord is near to those who are brokenhearted."

Psalm 34:17–18

As she sat there, Ashley began to understand that a difficult moment did not mean all her progress had been lost. Healing is not always a straight path, and old feelings can return even when meaningful change has taken place. She did not need to condemn herself for having another painful evening. She needed to respond with the same honesty and care she had been learning to practice: prayer, rest, truth, and support when necessary.

For a long time, Ashley had believed that strength meant keeping herself composed. Now she was discovering that vulnerability could be part of healing. She could admit that a memory still hurt without allowing it to control her future. She could cry without calling herself weak. She could ask for help rather than retreat into the belief that she had to manage every struggle alone.

"He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings you shall trust."

Psalm 91:4

The promise of God's covering did not make the tears disappear, but it reminded Ashley that she was not abandoned in them. Jesus was not present only on the days when she felt peaceful or confident. She could turn to Him in the middle of confusion, grief, and exhaustion. The refuge she had been learning to trust was still available, even when the journey became difficult again.

"Weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning."

Psalm 30:5

That night did not solve every problem, but it became another moment in which Ashley chose not to hide from what she felt. She allowed herself to rest, and when the next day came, she considered what support or practical steps might help her continue. A breakthrough is not always a dramatic event. Sometimes it is the quiet decision to keep moving toward healing rather than return to old patterns of silence and self-blame.

Looking back, Ashley could see that the breakdown had not erased the progress she had made. It revealed that there were still places in her heart that needed patience and care. The next step would be to recognize the signs of restoration without demanding that every day feel perfect. She was beginning to understand that strength could return gradually, and that even a difficult night could be met with hope.

To the reader walking through a breaking moment:

Tears do not mean you have failed, and a difficult day does not erase the steps you have already taken. Give yourself permission to be honest about what hurts. Reach toward Jesus, and when the weight feels too heavy, reach toward trustworthy support as well. Healing can include setbacks, but you do not have to face them alone. Take the next step with patience and grace.
