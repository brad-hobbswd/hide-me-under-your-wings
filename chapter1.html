Chapter 1: The Battle That Broke Her Strength

HomeTable of Contents

Ashley had become accustomed to being the person others could depend on. When someone needed advice, comfort, prayer, or a listening ear, she tried to make herself available. She wanted to be strong, and she did not want the people around her to worry about what she might be carrying. Over time, however, the effort to remain dependable began to conceal something important: she was a person with limits, needs, and wounds of her own. The strength others saw did not mean that she never struggled. It often meant that she had learned to keep struggling where few people could see.

At first, the pressure did not arrive as one overwhelming event. It gathered through the ordinary difficulties of life. Responsibilities demanded attention, expectations seemed to grow, and the needs of other people continued to arrive. Small stresses and disappointments accumulated until there was little room left for rest. Ashley kept moving because there were still things to do and people who needed her, but the peace she once knew was becoming harder to hold on to.

Work brought its own pressures. Expectations felt heavier, and the demands of each day seemed to leave less space to recover. Outside of work, personal concerns added another layer. Family disagreements, broken promises, and the disappointment of discovering that support was not always returned all took their toll. Even conversations that might once have felt ordinary could leave her emotionally drained. She could be surrounded by people and still feel as though no one understood what was happening inside her.

The hardest part was not always what other people could see. It was the private struggle of trying to make sense of hurt while continuing to meet the expectations of daily life. When someone has spent a long time being the strong one, admitting that they are struggling can feel like another burden. They may worry that speaking honestly will disappoint others, create conflict, or make them appear incapable. So they keep going, often without realizing how much of themselves they are setting aside in the process.

For Ashley, the weight eventually reached a point where she could no longer pretend that everything was fine. The hurt had not come from one isolated moment. It was the accumulation of pressure, conflict, disappointment, and the loneliness of feeling that she had to carry it all herself. She felt herself unraveling, not because she had stopped caring or because she lacked faith, but because she was exhausted. Sometimes the bravest thing a person can do is acknowledge that the burden has become too heavy to carry alone.

One evening, Ashley sat in her car and found that she could not bring herself to go inside. Her hands tightened around the steering wheel as the emotions she had been holding back finally surfaced. She cried, overwhelmed by the weight of everything she had tried to manage. There was no carefully prepared prayer and no polished explanation for what she was feeling. All she could offer in that moment was the truth.

"Lord Jesus, I cannot take another hit. I have nothing left."

Those words were not a declaration that she had given up on God. They were an honest expression of how deeply she needed Him. There are moments when prayer does not come in complete sentences. Sometimes it comes through tears, a whispered name, or the simple admission that we do not know what to do next. God is not asking us to disguise our weakness before we approach Him. He already knows the condition of our hearts, and we can come before Him without pretending to be stronger than we are.

That moment did not instantly resolve every conflict or remove every source of pain. The circumstances that had worn Ashley down still needed to be faced, and healing would take time. But something important had begun to change: she was no longer trying to carry the entire weight by herself. Her prayer became a turning toward Jesus, a recognition that her strength was limited but His faithfulness was not. Surrender did not mean that the struggle was imaginary. It meant that she was allowing God to meet her in the middle of it.

"He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings shall you trust."

Psalm 91:4

The words of Psalm 91 offered Ashley a picture of the refuge her heart needed. The image of being covered beneath the wings of the Lord speaks of care, closeness, and protection. It does not promise that a believer will never face another painful day. It reminds us that we can place our trust in God while we are still walking through difficult circumstances. His presence is not limited to the moments when life feels peaceful. He remains near when our hearts are troubled and our way forward is unclear.

As Ashley held on to that promise, the change came quietly. Her breathing began to settle, and she allowed herself to rest in the truth that she did not have to solve everything in that moment. The problems had not disappeared, but neither had the Lord. She could take the next step without demanding that she find the strength for every step ahead. The beginning of refuge was not found in having every answer. It was found in turning toward Jesus and trusting Him with the weight she could no longer carry alone.

There is an important difference between surrendering to hopelessness and surrendering our burdens to God. Hopelessness tells us that nothing can change and that we must face everything alone. Surrender to the Lord acknowledges our limits while placing our confidence in His character. It gives us permission to seek help, to pray honestly, to establish needed boundaries, and to take the next faithful step. Trusting God does not mean ignoring practical needs or pretending that painful situations do not matter. It means inviting Him into every part of our lives and allowing His truth to guide us.

To the reader who sees something of yourself in Ashley:

You may be carrying a burden that no one around you fully understands. You may have become so accustomed to helping others that asking for help feels unfamiliar. Perhaps you are tired of explaining your pain, or you have wondered whether anyone notices how much effort it takes just to get through the day. The Lord sees you. You do not have to wait until you have the right words or until your circumstances are settled before you come to Him.

Psalm 34:17 reminds us that the Lord hears the cry of the righteous and delivers them from their troubles. Bring Him the truth of what you are facing. Ask for wisdom for the decisions ahead, courage for the conversations you need to have, and strength for the steps you cannot yet see. If you need support from someone trustworthy, allow yourself to reach for it. You were never meant to prove your worth by carrying every burden alone.

Your weariness does not make you a failure, and your need for help does not make your faith less genuine. Let this be the place where you stop pretending that you are untouched by the struggle. Turn toward Jesus, one prayer and one step at a time. The storm may not end today, but you can begin learning to rest in the faithfulness of the One who walks with you through it.
