Chapter 3: When Words Become Weapons

HomeTable of ContentsBegin Reading

As Ashley began to find a measure of rest during her cave season, she hoped the worst of the emotional strain might finally be behind her. The quiet had helped her recognize how much she was carrying, and prayer had given her a place to bring feelings she had kept hidden. Yet healing did not remove every difficult relationship or erase every painful memory. Before long, she found herself facing another source of hurt: words spoken by someone she loved and trusted.

The conversations stayed with her long after they ended. Some words felt judgmental, while others seemed to be offered as correction but landed as rejection. Ashley replayed what had been said and wondered whether she had misunderstood, whether she had failed, or whether there was something about herself she needed to change. She wanted to respond, but she also feared making the situation worse. The pain was especially difficult because it came from someone whose opinion mattered to her.

"For it was not an enemy who reproached me... but it was you, a man my equal, my companion and my familiar friend."

Psalm 55:12–14

There is a particular ache when a relationship that once felt safe becomes a source of distress. Words from a stranger may be painful, but words from someone close can reach places that are difficult to protect. Ashley felt the old desire to retreat. She wanted to stop explaining herself, avoid further conflict, and disappear from conversations that left her questioning her worth. Yet she was beginning to learn that finding refuge in Jesus did not mean she would never be hurt again. It meant she could bring the hurt to Him before deciding how to respond.

In prayer, Ashley allowed herself to be honest. She did not pretend that the words had not wounded her, and she did not force herself to feel forgiveness before she was ready to begin the work of it. She asked Jesus to keep the pain from becoming bitterness and to give her wisdom about what needed to be addressed. Not every disagreement requires an immediate confrontation, but neither should the language of peace be used to excuse ongoing harm. Sometimes a wise response includes a truthful conversation, a clear boundary, or the help of someone trustworthy.

"The righteous cry out, and the Lord hears, and delivers them out of all their troubles."

Psalm 34:17

As Ashley continued praying, she began to separate the words she had heard from the truth of who she was. She could consider whether any correction was useful without accepting condemnation as her identity. She could acknowledge that a conversation had hurt without allowing that moment to define her entire life. Scripture became an anchor, not a way to deny her feelings, but a way to examine them in the light of God's truth.

She also began asking the Lord to help her release the desire to repay hurt with hurt. Forgiveness would not mean calling harmful words acceptable, pretending trust had never been damaged, or immediately restoring a relationship to the way it had been. It meant refusing to let resentment become the center of her heart. Rebuilding trust, where appropriate, would require honesty, changed behavior, and time. Ashley could pray for someone and still recognize the need for healthy boundaries.

That understanding marked an important step in her journey. The cave had taught her to bring hidden pain into God's presence; now she was learning to carry that same honesty into the way she handled relationships. She could not control what another person said, but she could seek God's wisdom for her response. The next part of her healing would involve more than surviving hurtful conversations. It would require learning what it meant to protect a wounded heart while remaining open to the healing Jesus was already beginning within her.

To the reader carrying pain from someone's words:

Words can leave wounds, especially when they come from someone you trusted. Give yourself permission to acknowledge what happened, seek wise counsel when needed, and ask God for clarity about the next step. You can forgive without denying the harm, and you can pursue peace without surrendering healthy boundaries. Your identity is not determined by another person's accusation or misunderstanding. Bring the pain to Jesus and let His truth guide your response.
